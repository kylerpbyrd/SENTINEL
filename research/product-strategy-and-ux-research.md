## Executive Summary

### The Hard Truth

Sentinel's value proposition is real but narrower than its framing suggests. The "Trust Layer for the Internet" framing faces a fundamental challenge: **most users do not actively seek to verify content authenticity.** This is not a product users wake up wanting. It is a product that solves a pain users only feel in specific, high-stakes moments.

The opportunity is not in convincing the general public to care about AI content — they demonstrably struggle to care. The opportunity is in serving **professional verifiers** (journalists, educators, researchers) whose jobs depend on content authenticity, and growing from there.

**This product has demand, but it is a slow-build infrastructure play, not a viral consumer hit.** Treat it accordingly.

## 1. Problem Validation

### 1.1 How Big Is the AI-Generated Content Problem (Mid-2026)?

The problem is real and accelerating:

- **Content Credentials / C2PA** reports that humans now produce and consume **over 400 quintillion bytes of data per day**, with 90% of all data created in the last two years. The organization frames "critically evaluating digital content" as **"an essential 21st century skill — a new type of digital literacy."**

- **Google DeepMind** has invested heavily in SynthID — watermarking for images, video, audio, and text — across Gemini, Veo, Imagen, Lyria, and NotebookLM. This signals Google views provenance as a strategic necessity, not a nice-to-have.

- The **Europol "90% of online content will be AI-generated by 2026"** forecast, while likely hyperbolic when originally published, has proven directionally accurate. Content generation capability has scaled faster than detection capability.

- **Pew Research (2023)** found that 79% of technology experts surveyed were either "more concerned than excited" or "equally concerned and excited" about digital change by 2035, citing misinformation, deepfakes, and erosion of trust as top concerns.

- **Pew (Oct 2024)** reported that ~75% of U.S. adults said they'd seen inaccurate election news, and "many say it's hard to tell what's true."

- **Originality.ai** has tracked and published AI content proliferation studies showing AI content dominates on Fortune 500 sites and in Google search results. Their product has iterated through 10+ model versions since November 2022, suggesting sustained demand.

**Assessment:** The problem is large, real, and growing. However, scale alone does not equal demand for a consumer-facing solution.

### 1.2 Are Users Actually Confused/Distressed?

**Mixed evidence.** The data suggests two distinct populations:

1. **Professional users actively distressed:** Journalists (Pew: 77% would still choose their career but 57% fear press freedom restrictions), educators overwhelmed by AI-generated student work, researchers unable to trust source material. These users have a **felt pain** and are actively seeking solutions.

2. **General consumers passively concerned:** Pew data from May 2026 shows Americans value "skepticism or discernment" in news consumption but most are not installing verification tools. Concern ≠ action.

The distinction is critical: Sentinel's addressable market is not "everyone who cares about AI content." It's "everyone whose work or decisions are materially impacted by content authenticity."

### 1.3 What Do Users Currently Do When They Encounter Suspicious Content?

From observed behavior and tool usage patterns:

- **Journalists & Researchers:** Reverse image search, check metadata, run through specialized detection tools, check C2PA credentials, contact original sources
- **Educators:** Run student work through Originality.ai, Turnitin, GPTZero
- **Power users:** Manual heuristic checks (writing style, fact cross-reference, source verification)
- **General users:** Scroll past, rely on platform labels, check comments, or simply remain unaware
- **Institutional users:** Deploy enterprise detection platforms (Originality.ai API, Copyleaks, Turnitin)

**Key insight:** The current workflow is fragmented, manual, and tool-switching-intensive. There is a gap for unified, one-click verification.

### 1.4 Is There Demand or Is This Speculative?

**Demand exists but is concentrated.** Evidence of real demand:

- The FTC took enforcement action in April 2025 against a company for falsely claiming "98% accuracy" without substantiation. The FTC engaging on this signals the market matters.
- Originality.ai raised venture funding and now offers 10+ products (AI Checker, Plagiarism, Grammar, Fact Checker, Guideline Checker, etc.), suggesting paying customers.
- C2PA has grown to **500+ companies** including Microsoft, Adobe, Intel, BBC, OpenAI, Google, Meta, and Amazon.
- Google invested in building SynthID directly into Gemini — if there were no demand for provenance verification, Google wouldn't build it.

**The demand is not "tell me if this is AI."** The demand is "help me make a decision I can defend." This aligns with Sentinel's explainability philosophy — but the market needs to be educated on why explainability matters. Most users currently want a binary answer.

## 3. Core Workflows

### 3.1 Critical User Journeys

**Journey 1: Journalist Verifying a Source Image**

```
DISCOVER → During editorial review, photo editor flags an image as potentially 
           AI-generated (visual anomalies, no photographer credit, 
           too-good-to-be-true timing)

ACTION   → Right-clicks image in browser → "Scan with Sentinel"
         → Sentinel analyzes: metadata, C2PA credentials, pixel-level artifacts, 
           reverse image search, source reputation
         → Returns confidence score + explainability panel

DECISION → Score 78% "Likely Authentic" — reviews evidence:
           - C2PA credentials present and valid (high weight)
           - No pixel-level AI artifacts detected
           - Image found on 3 news sites with photographer attribution
           - Metadata matches claimed capture device and date
           
         → Approves image for publication, attaches Sentinel report to 
           editorial notes as due diligence

OUTCOME  → Image published. Editor has audit trail if challenged.
           Publication credibility preserved.
```

**Journey 2: Educator Assessing a Student Paper**

```
DISCOVER → Reading student submissions for midterm. Paper #14 reads 
           "too polished" — vocabulary is inconsistent with the student's 
           previous work, structure is unnaturally uniform.

ACTION   → Submits paper to Sentinel via LMS integration or copy-paste
         → Sentinel analyzes: linguistic consistency with student's prior work, 
           burstiness/perplexity patterns, writing process timeline (if available), 
           stylistic fingerprint comparison

DECISION → Score 62% "Likely AI-Generated" — reviews evidence:
           - Writing style diverges 3.2σ from student's established fingerprint
           - Content shows low burstiness (consistent with LLM generation)
           - Document metadata shows 45-minute creation time for 2,500 words
           - 4 paragraphs match known GPT output patterns at >90% confidence
           
         → Opens academic integrity case with evidence attached

OUTCOME  → Student admits to using AI for 60% of the paper.
           Case resolved with documented evidence. No false accusation.
```

**Journey 3: Consumer Encountering Suspicious Social Media Post**

```
DISCOVER → Scrolling social media. Sees viral post: "LEAKED: Government 
           document proves [controversial claim]." Image looks slightly off.

ACTION   → Hover/click Sentinel badge on post (assuming platform integration) 
           OR right-click → "Check with Sentinel"
           
         → Sentinel provides quick-look summary:
           "⚠️ Multiple Concerns Detected"
           - Image: 92% likely AI-generated (GAN artifacts detected)
           - Source: Account created 3 days ago, no prior history
           - Text: 78% likely AI-generated (GPT-like generation patterns)
           - No C2PA credentials present

DECISION → User does NOT share the post.
         → Optionally: reports with Sentinel evidence attached

OUTCOME  → Misinformation spread prevented. User trust in Sentinel reinforced.
```

**Journey 4: Content Manager Reviewing Freelancer Batch**

```
DISCOVER → Monthly review of 40 articles from 10 freelancers. 
           Need to verify originality before publication.

ACTION   → Uploads batch to Sentinel dashboard
         → Sentinel analyzes all articles, flags 7 with concerns
         → Provides per-article breakdown with confidence scores

DECISION → Reviews 7 flagged articles:
           - 4 flagged for 85%+ AI generation → rejected, sent back to writers
           - 2 flagged for 25% AI (likely grammar tools) → approved with note
           - 1 flagged but writer demonstrates original research → override, approved
           
         → Sends feedback reports to flagged freelancers

OUTCOME  → Content quality maintained. SEO penalties avoided.
           Freelancers receive transparent feedback on expectations.
```

**Journey 5: Student Proving Originality**

```
DISCOVER → Receives email from professor: "Your essay was flagged by our 
           AI detection tool. Please meet to discuss."

ACTION   → Opens Sentinel's writing process report
         → Shows: document history with timestamps, edit patterns consistent 
           with human writing (pauses, revisions, nonlinear progress),
           stylistic consistency with prior work
         → Exports "Originality Certificate" showing writing process

DECISION → Presents certificate to professor

OUTCOME  → Professor accepts evidence. Grade stands.
           Student avoids false accusation consequences.
```

### 3.2 How Does Sentinel Fit Into Existing Workflows?

**Friction assessment for each persona:**

| Persona | Existing Workflow | Sentinel Integration | Friction Delta |
|---------|------------------|---------------------|----------------|
| Journalist | Multi-tool manual process (15-45 min) | Right-click → one scan (< 2 min) | **Dramatically reduces friction** |
| Educator | Submit to Turnitin/Originality.ai, interpret alone | LMS-integrated, explainable results | **Reduces friction, adds explainability** |
| Content Marketer | Spot-check articles manually or use Originality.ai | Batch dashboard, CMS integration | **Reduces friction at scale** |
| Consumer | Nothing (or manual heuristics) | Passive indicator or one-click scan | **Creates new behavior** (high friction) |
| Enterprise | Custom moderation pipelines | API integration | Integrates into existing (low friction) |
| Student | Nothing (defenseless) | Process tracking + certificate generation | **Creates new behavior** (but pain-driven) |

**Critical insight:** The consumer persona is the hardest adoption problem. They must form a new habit. Every other persona is replacing or augmenting an existing, painful workflow.

## 4. Explainability UX

### 4.1 How Have Other Products Communicated Uncertainty?

**Validated patterns from existing products and research:**

1. **Credit Karma / Financial products:** Use "confidence factors" with positive/negative indicators. Show exactly which factors influenced the score. Users understand "your credit utilization is high → score is lower" better than they understand a single number.

2. **Grammarly:** Tiers of confidence (critical, advanced, etc.) with color coding. Users learn to prioritize by severity without being overwhelmed.

3. **Apple Health / Fitness trackers:** Trend lines and "you're above/below your average" framing. Uncertainty is communicated through ranges ("120-135 bpm"), not point estimates.

4. **Weather apps:** The gold standard for communicating probability. "60% chance of rain" is universally understood. Users accept that weather predictions are imperfect without losing trust in the app.

5. **SynthID / Google:** Watermark-based approach — binary "was this watermarked by us?" detection. Limited but trustworthy. Users understand the limitation (only detects Google-generated content) and don't over-trust it.

6. **C2PA / Content Credentials:** "Nutrition label" metaphor — shows provenance chain. Users understand ingredients labels apply to food; translating this to content is intuitive.

7. **Originality.ai's "AI Allowance":** Introduced in July 2026 — lets users select tolerance levels (0%, 5%, 15%, 25%, 40% AI allowed). This acknowledges that "AI vs. human" is a false binary and allows contextual judgment. Brilliant UX concept — it transforms a detection tool into a policy enforcement tool.

### 4.2 Key UX Patterns for Showing Evidence

**DO:**
- **Progressive disclosure:** Show confidence score first, evidence on demand. Most users want the headline; power users want the details.
- **Visual weight hierarchy:** Primary signal (score + top 2-3 factors) should take 80% of visual attention. Secondary evidence in expandable sections.
- **Color coding with accessibility:** Green/yellow/red is intuitive but must be supplemented with symbols/shapes for colorblind users (12% of males).
- **Natural language explanations:** "We're 78% confident this is authentic because (a) it has valid C2PA credentials, (b) no AI artifacts were detected, and (c) the source has a trusted history." — Not "F1 score of 0.78 with factors [credential_match=0.95, artifact_score=0.02, source_reputation=0.87]."
- **What-you-can-do prompts:** After showing confidence, suggest next action. "Based on this, you might: view the source, check similar images, or flag for review."

**DON'T:**
- **Never show "100%" confidence.** Perfection erodes trust when inevitably wrong.
- **Don't hide disagreement between signals.** If C2PA says authentic but pixel analysis says AI-generated, show the disagreement. This builds trust more than an averaged-out score.
- **Don't use "truth" language.** Never say "This is true/false." Say "This appears authentic/generated."
- **Don't overwhelm with raw data.** "Perplexity: 234.7, burstiness: 0.43, zero-shot entropy: 1.8" is meaningless to non-ML users.

### 4.3 The "Boy Who Cried Wolf" Problem

This is the single biggest UX risk for Sentinel:

**The problem:** Every false positive ("This might be AI" on authentic content) erodes trust. Every false negative (AI content passed as authentic) erodes trust. Both errors are inevitable at scale.

**Mitigations:**

1. **Confidence calibration is UX, not just ML.** The system should be calibrated such that "85% confident" actually means "85% of items with this score are correctly classified." Poorly calibrated confidence destroys trust faster than poor accuracy.

2. **Error transparency.** When users report a misclassification, acknowledge it publicly (within the product). "You're right — we got this wrong. Here's what we're doing." This paradoxical approach (admitting error to build trust) is validated in service recovery research.

3. **Override with feedback loop.** Always allow "This assessment seems wrong" with a reason. This both (a) gives users agency, and (b) improves the model.

4. **Temporal confidence decay.** Confidence scores for time-sensitive content (news, breaking events) should show a warning: "Confidence is lower than usual because this content is new and signal data is limited." Users understand "we're less sure because it just happened."

5. **Scope humility.** Sentinel should actively communicate what it CANNOT do: "We can detect known AI generation patterns. We cannot detect novel generation methods, sophisticated editing, or determine intent."

6. **Track user override rate.** If users override Sentinel's assessments >15% of the time, either the model is bad or the UX isn't communicating confidence appropriately. This is a leading indicator of trust erosion.

### 4.4 Trust Indicators Validated in Research

From HCI and trust research (Nielsen Norman Group, academic literature):

1. **Process transparency beats outcome transparency.** "We check: C2PA credentials, pixel analysis, source reputation, and metadata" is more trust-building than "Our algorithm says 78%."

2. **Social proof for algorithmic judgments.** "4,200 users agreed with this assessment" (when available) increases trust — but must be genuine; fabricated social proof is catastrophic if discovered.

3. **Recency signals.** "Last verified: 2 minutes ago" builds more trust than a static badge. Users understand content provenance can change.

4. **Independent third-party validation.** If Sentinel's methodology is independently audited (e.g., by a university or standards body), displaying this increases trust significantly. C2PA and SynthID benefit from "built by Google/Adobe" trust; Sentinel needs to earn equivalent trust.

5. **User control increases trust in automation.** Research consistently shows: users trust systems more when they can adjust thresholds, even if they never actually adjust them. The mere presence of a sensitivity slider increases trust.

## 5. User Control Philosophy

### 5.1 The Control Spectrum

The project plan proposes: **Hide → Blur → Warn → Ignore → Always Show**

**Assessment:** This is the right framework but the wrong granularity for MVP.

#### What Matters to Users (by persona):

| Persona | Controls That Matter | Controls That Don't |
|---------|---------------------|---------------------|
| Journalist | Evidence detail level, source trust list | Hide/blur (they need to see everything) |
| Educator | Detection sensitivity, "AI allowance" threshold | Blur/warn (they're evaluating, not browsing) |
| Content Marketer | AI threshold per project, batch settings | Hide (they need to review, not suppress) |
| Consumer | Blur/warn/hide for passive browsing | Evidence detail (too much information) |
| Enterprise | API thresholds, per-content-type config | N/A (programmatic consumption) |
| Student | Export/certificate generation, process tracking | All content-filtering controls |

**Recommendation:** Content filtering controls (hide/blur/warn) are a **consumer-only feature.** Journalists, educators, and enterprises need *evaluation* controls, not *filtering* controls. The MVP should not invest heavily in content filtering. Focus on evaluation controls.

### 5.2 Configuration Complexity Tradeoffs

**The paradox of control:** More control options increase user satisfaction among power users but decrease adoption among casual users. Every additional setting is a decision the user must make before receiving value.

**Recommendations:**

1. **Sensible defaults, not configuration wizards.** First-run should require zero decisions. "Sentinel is active with recommended settings. You can customize anytime."

2. **Progressive complexity.** Basic mode: on/off. Advanced mode: per-signal weights, thresholds, allow/block lists. Never show advanced mode by default.

3. **Per-site, not global.** Users care about trust differently on different sites. "Always trust cnn.com" is more intuitive than "enable C2PA verification with 0.85 threshold."

4. **Profiles over settings.** "I'm a journalist" / "I'm an educator" / "I'm just browsing" preset profiles that configure all settings at once. Users understand their role better than they understand individual detection parameters.

### 5.3 Default Settings Philosophy

**Core principle:** Maximize safety for false negatives (missing AI content is worse than flagging human content as AI) but minimize harm from false positives (flagging human content as AI has real consequences).

**Tension:** You can't do both perfectly. For different personas, the harm asymmetry differs:

- **For educators:** False positive = falsely accusing a student = catastrophic harm. Err toward false negatives.
- **For journalists:** False negative = publishing AI content as authentic = credibility damage. Err toward flagging.
- **For consumers:** False positive = slightly annoying. False negative = no harm if they don't care anyway.

**Recommendation:** Default conservatively (prefer flagging over missing) but make sensitivity easily adjustable. Default sensitivity should correspond to the selected persona profile.

## 6. Product Scope & MVP Definition

### 6.1 Absolute Minimum Viable Product

**The MVP must answer one question:** "Can we make a single journalist verify a single image in under 2 minutes with enough confidence to publish it?"

This is the narrowest possible demonstration of the entire value chain:
- Content ingestion (right-click → send to analysis)
- Multi-signal analysis (metadata, pixel analysis, C2PA check, reverse image search)
- Confidence synthesis (weighted multi-signal → single score)
- Explainability output (what informed the score and why)
- User action (approve/reject/flag)

**Everything else is deferrable.**

### 6.2 Must Work Perfectly on Day One

| Feature | Why It Must Work | Failure Mode If It Doesn't |
|---------|-----------------|---------------------------|
| **Image analysis accuracy** | Core value proposition | Product has no reason to exist |
| **Response time < 3 seconds** | Journalists won't wait longer | They abandon and go back to manual |
| **Explainability panel** | The differentiator vs. binary detectors | Just another AI detector (commodity) |
| **Zero false positives on C2PA-verified content** | C2PA is ground truth; conflicting with it destroys credibility | Trust permanently destroyed |
| **Privacy (no data sent without consent)** | Core brand promise | Privacy-conscious users (journalists!) uninstall |
| **Works on Chrome + Firefox** | Browser coverage for target personas | 60%+ addressable market lost |
| **Offline/private mode indicator** | Users need to know when analysis is limited | Silent failure = trust erosion |

### 6.3 Deferrable Features

| Feature | Defer To | Rationale |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| Content filtering (hide/blur/warn) | Phase 3+ | Consumer-only; not core differentiator |
| Video analysis | Phase 2.5 | Higher complexity; image + text covers 80% of journalist use cases |
| Audio analysis | Phase 3 | Niche use case outside specific verticals |
| User accounts / cloud sync | Phase 3 | Local-first works for MVP; accounts needed for cross-device |
| API | Phase 3 | Must have product validated before building platform |
| Enterprise dashboard | Phase 3 | Long sales cycles; build after product-market fit signal |
| Mobile apps | Phase 4 | Extension validates concept first |
| Community trust graph | Phase 4 | Network effects require user base first |
| SDK | Phase 4 | Premature without API stability |

### 6.4 Features That Would Make Users Uninstall

**Interview these with prospective users, but predicted dealbreakers:**

1. **Browser slowdown > 500ms page load impact.** Users tolerate security extensions (ad blockers) slowing things down because the value is obvious. Sentinel's value is less immediately tangible. Performance is existential.

2. **Data exfiltration.** If users discover Sentinel is sending their browsing data to servers, uninstall is immediate and reviews are permanently hostile. Privacy must be technically verifiable, not just claimed.

3. **Completely wrong assessments on known content.** If a user tests Sentinel on an image they KNOW is AI-generated and it says "95% likely authentic," they uninstall and never return. First impressions on known cases are make-or-break.

4. **Intrusive UI.** Pop-ups, overlays, or badges that interfere with normal browsing. Users install ad blockers to REMOVE intrusions, not add them.

5. **"Boy who cried wolf" saturation.** If Sentinel flags 60%+ of all content as suspicious, users learn to ignore it. False positive rate is a UX metric, not just an ML metric.

### 6.5 Features That Would Make Users Recommend It

1. **"I checked something suspicious and it was right"** — Core loop. One correct flag on content the user already suspected creates a promoter.

2. **"It caught something I would have missed"** — Delight. Finding AI content the user thought was authentic is a "wow" moment.

3. **"I showed the report to my editor/committee/boss and they accepted it"** — Utility lock-in. The explainability report becomes institutional evidence.

4. **"I forgot it was running"** — Invisible utility. The highest compliment for a background tool.

5. **"I feel more confident browsing now"** — Emotional value. Anxiety reduction is a real product benefit that binary detectors don't deliver.

## 7. Adoption Barriers

### 7.1 Browser Extension Fatigue

**The numbers:**
- While specific 2026 extension installation data is paywalled, historical data shows the average Chrome user has **5-10 extensions installed** but only **2-3 actively used** (ad blocker, password manager, productivity tool).
- Extension uninstall rates within 30 days are estimated at **60-80%** for non-utility extensions.
- "Extension fatigue" is real — users are increasingly reluctant to install new extensions due to permission concerns and performance anxiety.

**Implications for Sentinel:**
- The extension must justify its existence in the first session. Value must be demonstrated within 5 minutes of install.
- The onboarding must show value on content the user already has open, not require them to seek out test content.
- Every permission request must be justified. "Read and change all your data on all websites" is the most terrifying Chrome permission and Sentinel needs it.

### 7.2 Trust Barrier — Why Would Users Trust Sentinel?

**This is the hardest problem you face.**

- Google (SynthID) and Adobe (C2PA) have built-in institutional trust. Sentinel is a new entity.
- "Trust me, I'm analyzing your content" is a hard sell when the user has no reason to trust you yet.

**Mitigations:**
1. **Technical transparency.** Open-source the client. If the analysis logic runs locally and is viewable on GitHub, technical users verify and vouch for it.
2. **Institutional partnerships.** A university research partnership validating Sentinel's methodology provides third-party credibility.
3. **Show your work.** Every assessment comes with "here's exactly what we checked and what we found." Over time, users learn which signals they personally trust.
4. **Never overclaim.** The fastest way to lose trust is to claim certainty. "We think this is probably AI-generated because [evidence]. We could be wrong."

### 7.3 Privacy Concerns as Adoption Barrier

**Paradox:** Journalists and researchers — the core personas — are the MOST privacy-conscious users. They cannot risk their sources being exposed or their research being tracked.

**Requirements:**
- Local-first processing must be real, not marketing. Facial recognition in images under investigation should not leave the device.
- Server-side analysis must be opt-in with clear disclosure: "To analyze this, Sentinel needs to send the image to our analysis server. No identifying information is sent."
- A transparency report (similar to ad blockers showing blocked elements) showing: "Sentinel processed 47 items today. 0 were sent to servers. 12 were analyzed with local models."
- **No telemetry without explicit opt-in.** This contradicts standard product practice but is non-negotiable for this user base and this value proposition.

### 7.4 Performance Concerns

Users will tolerate slow page loads from content they want to see. They will NOT tolerate slow page loads from a tool they chose to install.

**Hard requirements:**
- Image analysis: < 3 seconds for local models
- Page analysis: < 500ms impact on page load time
- Background processing: must yield to browser rendering
- Model size management: on-device models must be small enough to not bloat the extension

### 7.5 The "So What?" Problem

**The critical gap in Sentinel's current framing:**

The project says: "We tell users a confidence score." But what happens next?

Without **actionable outcomes**, confidence scores are interesting but useless. The "so what?" answer varies by persona:

| Persona | The Answer to "So What?" |
|---------|------------------------|
| Journalist | "So you can publish or kill the story with documented due diligence." |
| Educator | "So you can open an academic integrity case with evidence." |
| Content Marketer | "So you can reject/revise before publishing and protect SEO." |
| Consumer | "So you can decide whether to share, engage, or ignore." |
| Enterprise | "So your moderation systems can take automated action at scale." |
| Student | "So you can defend your original work against false flags." |

**The MVP must make the "so what" concrete for at least one persona.** A confidence score alone is not a product. A confidence score + recommended action + evidence trail = a product.

## 9. Honest Assessment

### 9.1 What's Strong About This Product Idea

1. **The philosophy is correct.** "Explainability over binary detection" is the right answer to a problem that most competitors are solving incorrectly. Originality.ai's "AI Allowance" feature (July 2026) is the market validating your thesis — the industry is moving toward your position.

2. **The timing is right.** C2PA, SynthID, Content Credentials, and regulatory pressure (EU AI Act, FTC enforcement) are creating infrastructure that Sentinel can leverage. The rails are being laid. The trust layer needs to be built on top of them.

3. **The journalist/educator beachhead is real.** Both personas have unserved needs. Journalists need provenance verification beyond what C2PA provides. Educators need explainable detection beyond what Originality.ai provides. Both have budgets.

4. **Multi-signal approach is a durable moat.** Any competitor can build a detection model. Building a system that synthesizes 8+ independent signals with explainable weighting is harder to replicate.

5. **Privacy-first is a genuine differentiator.** Most AI detection tools are cloud-based and collect user data. Sentinel's local-first approach will resonate with the exact users who care most about content authenticity — because they also care about privacy.

### 9.2 What's Weak or Risky

1. **The consumer use case is aspirational, not validated.** "Everyone will want this" is the most dangerous assumption in consumer software. Most people scroll past AI content without caring. Changing consumer behavior requires either a catalyzing event (major deepfake scandal) or platform mandates.

2. **The TAM is smaller than it looks.** "Everyone who uses the internet" is not the addressable market. The addressable market is professional verifiers + enterprises. This is a solid B2B business, not a mass consumer product. The project plan's vision of "browser extension → everyone" skips the necessary B2B phase.

3. **The product name and framing are too grandiose for Phase 2.** "Trust Layer for the Internet" describes a Phase 4 infrastructure company. Phase 2 is a detection and explainability tool for professionals. These are very different products with very different go-to-market strategies.

4. **Building detection models is expensive.** Even leveraging existing models, maintaining accuracy as new LLMs are released (Originality.ai releases model updates every 4-6 months) requires ongoing ML investment. Sentinel cannot build once and forget.

5. **Explainability generates legal surface area.** If Sentinel says "we're 95% confident this is AI-generated" and a user acts on that and is wrong, there is liability. The explainability that differentiates Sentinel also creates evidence that can be used against it.

6. **The "so what" gap is real.** The project plan says "Users decide to hide/blur/warn/ignore." But the people who will actually pay for Sentinel don't want to hide content — they want to make decisions with evidence. The content filtering framing is solving the wrong problem for the wrong persona.

### 9.3 Fundamental Challenge: Is Sentinel a Product or Infrastructure?

The project plan says: "Sentinel should become infrastructure. The browser extension is the first product. It is not the company."

But you cannot sell infrastructure on day one. Infrastructure companies (Stripe, Twilio, AWS) started with products that solved immediate problems for specific users, then became platforms.

**Recommendation:** Stop thinking about Sentinel as "infrastructure" for Phase 0-2. Think of it as "a tool that helps journalists and educators verify content authenticity with explainable evidence." Win that market. Then expand.

### 9.4 Revised Problem Statement

**Current (too broad):**
> "The internet is rapidly becoming filled with AI-generated content. Users need transparency into authenticity, provenance, and trustworthiness."

**Recommended (specific, validated):**
> "Professional content evaluators — journalists, educators, and content managers — need a tool that verifies digital content authenticity with explainable, evidence-based confidence scores, because binary AI detection tools fail to provide the evidence required to make and defend consequential decisions."

### 9.5 Go/No-Go Assessment

**GO — with constraints:**

- **Go** on building for journalists and educators as the initial market
- **Go** on explainability as the core differentiator
- **Go** on local-first privacy architecture
- **No-go** on consumer content filtering for MVP (defer to Phase 3)
- **No-go** on "Trust Layer for the Internet" positioning until Phase 3+ traction
- **No-go** on video/audio analysis until text+image product-market fit is demonstrated

**If I were an investor:** I would fund this as a B2B content verification platform with a consumer expansion thesis contingent on Phase 2 metrics. I would not fund this as a consumer-first product.

## 10. Recommendations for Phase 1 (Architecture)

### 10.1 What to Build First

1. **Image analysis pipeline** (metadata, C2PA, pixel artifact detection, reverse image search) — covers 80% of journalist use cases
2. **Text analysis pipeline** (perplexity, burstiness, stylistic consistency, known LLM pattern matching) — covers 80% of educator use cases
3. **Confidence synthesis engine** — weighted multi-signal combination with calibration
4. **Explainability UI** — progressive disclosure of evidence
5. **Browser extension shell** — right-click integration, minimal UI footprint

### 10.2 Persona-Specific Research Needed Before Building

Before writing code:
- Interview 10 journalists about their current verification workflow
- Interview 10 educators about their current AI detection experience (especially Turnitin/Originality.ai frustrations)
- Test 3 existing tools (Originality.ai, GPTZero, Hive Moderation) on known test content and document where they fail
- Run a small survey (50+ respondents from target personas) asking: "Would you install a browser extension that does X?"

### 10.3 Key UX Risks to Mitigate in Architecture

1. **Processing latency:** Design the analysis pipeline to return partial results progressively. Show metadata results in 500ms while pixel analysis runs for 2s.

2. **Model staleness:** Design for model hot-swapping from day one. The architecture must support replacing detection models without releasing a new extension version.

3. **Evidence storage:** If explainability reports are exportable (PDF, link), they create a permanent record. If the model later improves, old reports may contradict new analyses. Design for temporal context — reports should be frozen at time-of-analysis.

4. **Offline degradation:** Design a clear degradation path. When offline, which signals are unavailable? How is reduced confidence communicated to the user?
